01
Native Windows app · free and open source

A second-monitor map for Escape from Tarkov.

Your active quests' objectives, the extracts and your position on the map of the raid you're in. What you'd otherwise look up in the wiki, in one window.

Quest status comes from the game's own log files. Your position comes from the name of the screenshot the game saves when you press your screenshot key. No macros, no input to the game, nothing read from its memory, nothing drawn over it.

Get it on GitHub Free and open source (MIT). Windows 10 and 11.

03
Before the raid

Pick the map that gets the most done.

Maps ranked by how many of your quests they finish or move on. What to bring, and where to get it: trader, flea, barter or loose on the map.

Pick the quests you want to do, and they come first: on the map and in what to bring. When the raid starts loading, Shturmap shows the kit to check, while you can still cancel.

Quest cards

The quest on one card.

Objectives, keys and items for any quest, one hover away. Pick a quest and it comes first in the raid card, with live distances. The wiki is one click away for the rest.

Quest tracking

Quests track themselves.

Started, finished, failed: Shturmap reads it from the game's own log files. Nothing has to be ticked off.

The logs don't say when a single objective is done, so you can tick one on its quest's card if you want the map to stop leading you there.

Quests you started before your oldest log aren't known.

Questions

What players ask.

New to Tarkov, or to Shturmap

What is Shturmap, and what isn't it?

A second window for Escape from Tarkov. Before a raid: which map gets the most of your active quests done, and what to bring. In the raid: the map with your objectives, the extracts and the spot where you took your last screenshot.

It isn't a live position: your marker shows where you were when you last pressed your screenshot key. It shows nothing about other players, and it does nothing in the game for you.

How does it know where I am?

When you press your screenshot key, the game saves a screenshot and writes your position and facing into its file name. Shturmap reads that name and moves your marker there. If the picture shows the game's extract list at its top right, Shturmap also reads that list, on your PC, to tell which extracts are yours this raid; you can turn that off in settings. It reads which key is yours from the game's settings.

Nothing else moves the marker. Press the key again when you want a fresh position; an old one is marked with its age.

PvE or PvP?

Both, and the seasonal mode. Shturmap reads the mode from the game's log and loads that mode's quests and data. For PvP, see the question about fairness below.

Which maps does it cover?

The maps tarkov.dev covers. The Lab, Labyrinth and Icebreaker are drawn from tarkov.dev's top-down renders; when those can't be loaded, as a plain 10 m grid with your position, objectives and extracts.

Does it show the story chapters?

Not yet. Its quests come from tarkov.dev, which doesn't publish the story chapters (Falling Skies, Batya and the others), so Shturmap shows the traders' quests only.

Does it work in my language?

Quest, map and item names follow your game's language, from tarkov.dev; where tarkov.dev has no texts in it, they are in English. Shturmap's own words are English.

What does it cost, and what does it send?

Nothing. It is free and open source (MIT).

It sends nothing about you or your game unless you send a report or allow crash reports. Its other traffic downloads public data and map artwork, and asks GitHub whether there's a newer Shturmap (you can turn that off). What a report holds and who receives it is in the privacy notice.

One thing comes close to your position. The Lab, Labyrinth and Icebreaker are loaded from tarkov.dev's image service piece by piece, as you look at them. In a raid the part displayed is usually the part you are in, so those requests can show roughly where on the map that is, to tarkov.dev's image service and to no one else. No account, quest list or position is sent with them.

What do I need, and how do I install it?

64-bit Windows 10 (version 2004 or later) or Windows 11. Download Shturmap-Setup.exe from the GitHub releases and run it: it installs for your Windows user only, without admin rights. The installer isn't signed, so Windows warns about it; click More info, then Run anyway.

Shturmap keeps itself up to date. It checks at start and every 6 hours, downloads in the background and applies the update the next time you start it, never during a raid. Settings (the gear at the top right) → Updates switches that to Tell me only or Off.

It finds the game by itself; if it doesn't, choose the game folder in settings. Without the game on the PC you can still browse the maps. To remove Shturmap, use Windows Settings → Apps, or Uninstall Shturmap in its settings, which can delete its data too.

How do I learn how it works?

A short tour starts the first time you open Shturmap, outside a raid: seven chapters over the window, about a minute and a half, with an example raid on Customs. It shows what Shturmap reads, how it follows the game, how to pick quests, the screenshot key that puts you on the map, and what the raid card tells you. Esc ends it at any point; help (the ? at the top right, or F1) brings it back with Take the tour.

Something's wrong or missing?

Tell us from the app: the feedback button at the top right. No account needed, and you can see exactly what's sent before you send it. Shturmap is in private testing, so expect rough edges.

With a GitHub account you can also open an issue on GitHub. Issues are public: don't post the game's logs, screenshots or ids from your game there. The app's report leaves those out.

For experienced players

What does it read, and what does it never do?

It reads the file names of your screenshots and, of one taken in a raid, the top right corner of its picture for the game's extract list; the game's application and push-notification logs, Control.ini and Game.ini, where the game is installed (from Windows, Steam and the launcher's settings), and public data from tarkov.dev; the list is under Boundaries. It never reads or writes the game's memory, never opens the game's process from its own code (its installer is the one exception), sends no keystrokes or mouse input, doesn't capture your screen or draw over the game, and changes no game files.

Is this allowed? Can I get banned?

Battlestate hasn't said anything about tools that read screenshot names, for or against, and we found no verified ban for using one, though tools like this have been around since 2022. That is our research as of 3 October 2026, not a promise.

Battlestate's License Agreement (section 4.3.4) forbids outside software that "captures, collects, counts or otherwise 'retrieves' information reproduced or stored by" the game, unless Battlestate permits it at its own discretion, and it hasn't permitted any tool like this. Read literally, that covers every companion app, Shturmap included. The same words speak against tools players have used for years: TarkovMonitor, by the makers of tarkov.dev, has watched the game's log files and read positions from screenshots since 2022, and RatScanner has taken a screenshot to identify an item since 2020. Neither has Battlestate's permission either. So there is no guarantee against sanctions, for them or for Shturmap: use it at your own risk.

Shturmap is an unofficial fan project, not made or endorsed by Battlestate Games.

Where do the coordinates come from?

From the game. It writes them into every screenshot's file name, and Battlestate's own support article on reporting a bug says so: "The name of the screenshot contains the coordinates of the place where the screenshot was taken." That is the source of the coordinates, not an endorsement of Shturmap.

Isn't a map with my position unfair in PvP?

Some players think so; others compare it to keeping a map open on a second screen. Shturmap shows only where you were when you last pressed your screenshot key, and only to you. It is at its most useful for learning maps and in PvE.

Does it share my position, or take screenshots for me?

No. Your position stays with you: Shturmap never shares it with your squad or anyone else. And it never takes a screenshot for you: no timer, no automatic key press. Your marker moves only when you press your screenshot key.

What does the installer do with other running programs?

Shturmap is installed, updated and removed by Velopack, an open-source installer that comes with it. While it installs Shturmap, applies an update or uninstalls it, Velopack goes through the programs running on the PC to close running copies of Shturmap. To do that it asks Windows for a handle to each running program, with the rights to read where its file is and to end it, and it ends only programs that run from Shturmap's own folder.

If the game is running at that moment, it is asked about like every other program; nothing is read from it, written to it or sent to it. To keep even this away from a running game, install Shturmap and let it update while the game is closed: under Updates in settings, Tell me only lets you choose the moment.

Does it change or delete any of my files?

No, with one exception you turn on yourself. Delete position screenshots in settings is off unless you tick it; ticked, each screenshot that gave a position is deleted 5 seconds after its name was read, for good. Screenshots already in the folder, and screenshots from the menus, stay.

Can I check what it does?

Yes. The code is open source on GitHub under the MIT licence, and its tests run on every push, in public. One fails if Shturmap's own code ever uses the Windows functions for opening or reading another program, sending input, hooking or capturing the screen; another checks that it downloads data and artwork from tarkov.dev and GitHub's raw files only.

Those check the source code. What ties your download to it: from 0.4.0, each release is built and published on GitHub's servers by the repository's own workflow, which records a signed build provenance for the Setup and its packages. With the GitHub CLI, signed in: gh attestation verify Shturmap-Setup.exe -R shturmap/shturmap. The release also carries the Setup's SHA-256, to compare with Get-FileHash Shturmap-Setup.exe.

It installs for your Windows user, without admin rights. Windows Sandbox (Windows Pro, Enterprise or Education) is one way to try a program away from your own Windows; we haven't tried Shturmap in it yet. More in the README, under Check it yourself.

Is this another vibe-coded app?

It is written with AI, and it is fair to ask. Most of the code comes from an AI coding assistant (Claude, by Anthropic), credited as co-author in the commits, working for one maintainer who plays the game, decides what the app does and how it looks, and uses it.

What sets it apart from code nobody looked at: the decisions are written down with their reasons in a design document the code has to follow, and more than 1,100 automated tests check it. One of them fails if Shturmap's own code ever uses the Windows functions for opening or reading another program, sending input, hooking or capturing the screen.

None of this makes it free of faults: it is in private testing and has rough edges. You don't have to take our word for it either. The code is open, and what it reads and never does is listed under Boundaries.

What if Battlestate takes the coordinates out of the screenshot names?

Then Shturmap shows no position, and the rest keeps working: maps ranked by what you can get done, what to bring, quest cards, and the map with your objectives and the extracts.
